# TASK-REPORT t20: part two design record

## What I built

- `docs/d2/part-two-design.md`: the decision record for the owner. It covers
  five decisions: transport, private key, announcement, same repository and
  base commit. Each one gives the options, a recommendation, the probe behind
  it with its command and results, the cost, and what would change it. The
  record also has a table of detection times, a list of what loopback could
  not show (NOT VERIFIED, for t28), a table of what each task from t21 to t28
  takes from the record, an owner approval section (`Status: pending`), and
  one closing list of 9 open questions that can each be answered in a line.
- `spikes/d2-part-two/`: four probes that use Node's standard library only,
  plus their evidence. No npm dependency was added and `package.json` is
  unchanged.
  - `transport-probe.mjs`: TLS 1.3 authenticated in both directions, using a
    self-signed certificate derived in memory from an Ed25519 key, and a
    trust list.
  - `key-probe.mjs`: environment, PEM, file descriptor 3 and file delivery of
    the key, and what `/proc` still shows afterwards.
  - `announce-probe.mjs`: announcements every 5 seconds, a 15 second silence
    threshold, and stop, crash, halt, resume, duplicate-name and
    untrusted-key cases.
  - `repo-probe.mjs`: root commit and origin as identity candidates, a named
    commit check, and thin and full bundles.
  - Shared helpers: `lib.mjs`, `core.mjs` (a stand-in core) and `proc.mjs`.
    `README.md` gives the commands.
  - Evidence in `evidence/*.ndjson`, holding booleans, timings and short
    commit ids, never a key.
- `tests/test_part_two_design.py`: 24 tests that hold the record to the
  acceptance criteria. They check:
  - every section and part is present;
  - each cited probe and evidence file exists;
  - the evidence shows mutual refusal, no secret on the wire, and no key in a
    worker;
  - the stated seconds equal the numbers the announcement probe ran with,
    and every case met its bound;
  - a detection table exists;
  - NOT VERIFIED items are marked for t28;
  - the open questions are one-line items in the last section;
  - an approval status is present;
  - the record holds no IP address other than loopback, no host-like name,
    no path under `/home` and no per-user dotfile path, and the evidence
    holds no key.

## Choices and assumptions (not measured)

- I treated the spec's resolved decisions as fixed and did not offer them as
  options: key pairs, the operator adding keys on each host, revocation by
  removal, direct announcement between cores, and one user per host.
- I read "where the private key lives" as a choice between delivery paths.
  I recommend grant injection, as spec h27 says, and record the measured cost:
  the key stays in `/proc/<pid>/environ` for processes of the same user.
  Open question 3 lets the owner switch to a file.
- I chose node names `host-a` and `thor` in the probes as role labels.
  `spikes/` is exempt from the example-node check, and the record itself
  names only `thor`.
- The contract consequences for t23 and t26 come from reading the MINOR rule
  in `contract/README.md`, not from a probe. They are: a new member-to-member
  submit route, because `repo` is a required absolute path in 3.0; and a
  sensitivity mark that would be MAJOR as a new field on a 3.0 shape.
- The `/proc` residue under K1 was measured. That the OpenShell sandbox
  cannot see the host's `/proc` is an assumption, recorded as NOT VERIFIED.
